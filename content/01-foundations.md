# A quick check before you click

A website can make a choice easy to find, easy to miss, or hard to undo. A dark pattern uses that design to steer you toward something you did not intend, such as an extra purchase, a subscription, or sharing more information.

Start with what you came to do. If you want to cancel, a pause is a different outcome. If you want one product, an added warranty is another purchase. Keeping that distinction clear makes it easier to notice when a page changes the question.

## Check the defaults

Before you submit a form or pay, look for checked boxes and selected options. Read the cart line by line rather than checking only the total. Look for extra services, recurring billing, and choices hidden under “More options.”

Imagine buying a $40 item and finding a $19 warranty selected for you. Remove it if you do not want it, then check that both the line item and the total have changed. Save a screenshot if the choice or charge could later be disputed.

## Read what the buttons actually do

A large, bright button often feels like the obvious next step. That does not tell you whether it serves your goal. Read its label and the nearby alternatives.

“Pause,” “downgrade,” and “cancel” mean different things. “Store credit” is not the same as money returned to the original payment method. If the wording is unclear, stop and look for an explanation before confirming.

## Check the timing

For a trial or subscription, find the renewal date, price, and cancellation instructions before signing up. Put any reminder you need in your calendar. Do not rely on remembering a term that was briefly shown during checkout.

## Confirm the result

After making your choice, look for a confirmation page, email, or updated account setting. Save the confirmation with the date. If a page says a request was received, check whether that also means the requested action is complete.

You do not need to investigate every page. A useful habit is simply to check the defaults, read the wording, and confirm that the result matches what you intended.
