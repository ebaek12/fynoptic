# A record you can actually use

A useful record lets you explain what happened without relying on memory. Keep it short enough to find later and detailed enough to connect your request to the relevant purchase, setting, or account.

## Save the important screens

Capture the choice you were offered, the price or terms that mattered, and the confirmation of your action. For an unexpected cart item, save the line item and total. For a cancellation, keep the request and the confirmation or response.

Give files names you can recognize later, such as “2026-09-14_subscription_cancellation.” Put related messages and screenshots together.

## Include dates and context

Record when you took the action and how you contacted the company. Keep reference numbers when they are available. Distinguish what the page said from what you expected it to mean.

“Sent a cancellation request by email on September 14” is more useful than “I cancelled a while ago.” If you are still waiting for confirmation, say so in your record.

## Keep private information out

Do not put passwords or full payment-card numbers in your evidence record. Before sharing a screenshot, create a copy with sensitive information removed. Keep the original somewhere private if you need it for your own records.

## Decide what to do next

Write down the outcome you want and the next step you plan to take. That might be checking an account setting, asking for written confirmation, or following up on an earlier request. Use the company’s published instructions to find the right contact route.
