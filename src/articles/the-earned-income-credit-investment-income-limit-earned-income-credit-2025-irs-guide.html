This guide covers “The Earned Income Credit Investment-Income Limit” within earned income credit. The following material presents the IRS treatment of this topic, including the qualifications, exceptions, and examples in the source section. Read the rules alongside the facts of your own situation before deciding what to report, deduct, or document.

Adapted from Publication 596 (2025), Earned Income Credit (EIC), published by the Internal Revenue Service. This guide retains the source edition’s years, amounts, deadlines, and examples. It is guidance for that edition, not a statement that the same figures apply to every tax year.

Context for these rules

This chapter discusses Rules 1 through 7. You must meet all seven rules to qualify for the EIC. If you don’t meet all seven rules, you can’t get the credit and you don’t need to read the rest of the publication.

If you meet all seven rules in this chapter, then read either chapter 2 or chapter 3 (whichever applies) for more rules you must meet.

The Earned Income Credit Investment-Income Limit

You can’t claim the EIC unless your investment income is $11,950 or less. If your investment income is more than $11,950, you can’t claim the credit.

Use Worksheet 1 in this chapter to figure your investment income.
