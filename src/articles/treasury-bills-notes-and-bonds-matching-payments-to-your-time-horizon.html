Treasury bills, notes, and bonds all represent federal debt, but their maturity schedules and interest-payment patterns differ. Those differences affect when you receive cash and how long the security is designed to remain outstanding. This guide compares the main fixed-principal Treasury products, including short-term cash management bills. It helps readers distinguish a product's maturity from a plan to sell it early and understand which purchases are available through TreasuryDirect.

Separate an interest payment from the return of principal

A bill and a coupon-paying security can produce cash at different times even when their quoted yields look similar. A bill generally pays its face amount at maturity; the difference between its purchase price and face amount is the interest. A note or bond makes periodic interest payments, while principal is due at maturity. Receiving a coupon does not mean the original investment has become available to spend.

For a practical comparison, write a timeline showing the date of purchase, each expected cash payment, and the final maturity date. Place the expense you are saving for on the same timeline. If that expense occurs before maturity, you would need another source of cash or would have to sell the security. The maturity date is therefore more useful than the broad product name when planning a specific expense.

Also distinguish ordinary Treasury bills from cash management bills. The latter are issued to meet particular financing needs and do not follow the same regular term pattern. Check the announcement and the purchase channel rather than assuming every short-term Treasury issue can be bought through the same TreasuryDirect screen.

Treasury Bills

We sell Treasury Bills (Bills) for terms ranging from four weeks to 52 weeks.

Bills are sold at a discount or at par (face value). When the bill matures, you are paid its face value.

You can hold a bill until it matures or sell it before it matures.

Note about Cash Management Bills: We also sell Cash Management Bills (CMBs) at various times and for variable terms. Cash Management Bills are only available through a bank, broker, or dealer. We do not sell them in TreasuryDirect.

Treasury Bonds

We sell Treasury Bonds for a term of either 20 or 30 years.

Bonds pay a fixed rate of interest every six months until they mature.

You can hold a bond until it matures or sell it before it matures.

Treasury Bonds are not the same as U.S. savings bonds

EE Bonds, I Bonds, and HH Bonds are U.S. savings bonds. For information, see U.S. Savings Bonds.

Putting this into practice

Choose the cash-flow dates you need before comparing product labels. A security that matures after your spending deadline may require an earlier market sale. Review how interest is paid, where you can purchase the issue, and how its term fits the money's intended purpose.
